ENFORCEMENT INTERNALS

The full path from "user taps TikTok" to "Sonder shows a card table" — and back.

The detection → gate flow

1. Target app launches; its window appears
2. SonderAccessibilityService receives TYPE_WINDOW_STATE_CHANGED
   (filtered: targets only, self/system/notification sources ignored,
   debounced against event floods)
3. EnforcementCoordinator asks GateDecider about the package's bank:
   enabled AND bank > 0 → PASS/GRANTED, enabled AND bank = 0 → GATE
4. If gated: GateOverlayHost raises a full-screen SYSTEM_ALERT_WINDOW
   instantly (covers the target's first frame) and draws the table on it
5. The blocker going up over a short-form surface pauses the clip behind it
   (Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts, Facebook Reels/Stories)
6. Hand played → the stake settles into AccessPolicy
   · win  → bank += stake, clamped to the target's max
   · loss → bank -= stake, floored at 0
7. A winning hand drops the overlay; the app is simply open
8. While the banked app is in front, each pass refreshes lastSeenMillis
   and drains the bank by the elapsed gap (clamped to 10 s). Nothing is
   billed while the app is not in front, and nothing revokes a bank.
9. Each pass also sweeps for a gated app left floating in
   picture-in-picture: paused and covered like any other surface

Why the overlay layer exists

Starting an activity from the background is restricted on modern Android, and even a fast Activity launch shows a frame or two of the target app first. The overlay is drawn the instant the blocked window is detected — before the target's content is meaningfully visible — and the gate is drawn inside it, so there is no second window to bring up. If the overlay permission is missing, Sonder has nothing to cover the target with and the gate cannot appear at all.

The 10-second permission audit

Accessibility can be "on" in Settings yet not yet connected when an app cold-starts — a well-known Android quirk. Sonder schedules its audit 10 seconds after launch, not at launch: by then the service binding has settled, so the check reflects reality. Missing pieces raise a pixel-styled popup with deep links straight into each Settings page, and the audit re-runs when you return from Settings.

Why absolute epoch millis

Every stored time — the bank's lastSeenMillis, the moment it emptied, the timestamp on a hand — is an absolute instant, never a countdown. Combined with a bank that only moves when the app is in front, that means there are no alarms left to fire:

  • Force-kill Sonder mid-hand? The hand is gone; the bank is not.
  • Reboot? BootReceiver warms the bank cache and nothing more.
  • Doze? Nothing to fire — the drain happens when the user opens the app, not on a schedule.

Known limitations

  • Scope is per-app or per-surface — a Reels-only target cannot be scoped to one tab of Reels.
  • The short-form detector reads view identifiers, so a rename in a shipping app can blind it until the marker list catches up.
  • Picture-in-picture is recognised by window size, so an unusually small or large float can be missed.
  • Aggressive OEM battery managers can suspend the service — survival guide.
  • Release APKs fall back to the Android debug key until signing secrets are added — sideloadable, but not Play-publishable.
