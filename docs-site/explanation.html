WHY BLACKJACK?

Sonder's premise: the cost of opening a distracting app should be paid in attention, not in money or in a notification you can swipe away.

Friction as a feature

Most screen-time tools rely on willpower at the moment of temptation — the exact moment you have the least of it. Sonder moves the cost earlier: to get in, you must play a hand. Losing doesn't just fail to reward you; it takes back time you were already holding. Every minute in the app has to be won first, so "one quick scroll" becomes a hand you have to sit through, and the scroll you win has a visible end.

Why a bank rather than a lockout

A hard lockout after a loss is a dead end: it teaches nothing and invites uninstalling. An empty bank is not a lockout — it is a price, and the price is paid in time you put at risk rather than in time invented for you. Every win buys time that is yours: it sits in the bank until you spend it, and only the minutes you actually use are deducted. That is the difference that matters. A wall-clock grant punishes you for leaving; a bank rewards you for being deliberate, because walking away with 29 minutes is walking away with 29 minutes.

The betting is what keeps it from being a grind. Small chips make a bad streak cheap; all-in turns a good run into an evening, and the ceiling you set is what stops a good run from becoming an unbounded one. This mirrors commitment devices in behavioral economics: a cost that is escapable through effort sustains motivation, while a cost escapable only by quitting the tool destroys it.

Why chance, not a quiz or a hold

  • Quizzes become speed-runnable; you pattern-match the answers in three days.
  • Timed holds are pure dead time; you wait them out on the lock screen and the habit loop stays intact.
  • A hand of chance can't be grooved into muscle memory. Each gate session is genuinely uncertain, so the "maybe I'll just lose and be locked out" calculus stays honest.

Blackjack specifically is fast (~10 seconds per hand), has clean rules that fit a phone screen, and — unlike loot-box mechanics — the chips are minutes you already won, not money. There is no buy-in and no purchase: nothing here is bought, sold, or won of monetary value, which matters for both ethics and Play Store review.

The honesty problem

No enforcement app can survive a determined user — clear data, disable accessibility, uninstall. Sonder doesn't pretend otherwise. The design target is the middle of the night version of you: enough friction that auto-pilot doesn't get through, without requiring heroics from the morning version who has to set it up. Targets are chosen once, on purpose, and the reset procedure is documented (guides → reset) precisely so the honest path is as easy as the dishonest one.
